## KQ1. Multi-language audio tracks: eligibility, rollout, upload, discovery effect

### Takeaway
As of Oct 2026 multi-language audio (uploading your own dubbed tracks) has been rolled out "to all creators" since Sept 2025 per press, but the official Help page still gates it behind "Advanced features" and says access is expanding gradually; official pilot data says 15–25%+ of watch time on dubbed videos came from non-primary languages. There is **no official statement** that a Japanese video with an English track is surfaced in US recommendations; only vendor claims that playback defaults to the viewer's preferred language.

### Cited Findings
- Official Help page "Add Multi-language features to your videos" (answer/13338784): multi-language audio is described as currently open to a subset of creators who have Advanced features, with access expanding gradually; it also states (excerpt) "Multi-language audio lets you upload your own dubbed audio tracks. It does not automatically create these tracks, unlike the automatic dubbing feature." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/13338784?hl=en)
- A third-party guide that says it checked YouTube Help on 2026-10-03 still lists Advanced-features access as the current gate for uploaded second audio tracks and advises confirming in your own Studio account — [BeMultilingual (third-party)](https://www.bemultilingual.ca/blog/youtube-dubbing-updates)
- Press (TechCrunch, 2025-09-10): YouTube launched multi-language audio for all creators after a two-year pilot, rolling out "over several weeks"; the pilot began in 2023 with MrBeast, Mark Rober, Jamie Oliver — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/); [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/9/youtube-expands-its-multi-language-audio-feature-to-all-creators-globally)
- Press: YouTube declined to share how it was determining which creators were eligible and did not say when the option would be broadly available (at the time of the expansion) — [AlternativeTo](https://alternativeto.net/news/2025/9/youtube-expands-its-multi-language-audio-feature-to-all-creators-globally)
- YouTube-reported pilot results (via press): creators who uploaded multi-language audio tracks saw "over 25% of their watch time coming from views in the video's non-primary language"; Jamie Oliver's channel reportedly tripled views — [Storyboard18](https://storyboard18.com/how-it-works/youtube-rolls-out-multi-language-audio-feature-bringing-video-dubbing-to-all-creators-80746.htm); [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-is-helping-creators-reach-global-audiences-thanks-to-multi-language-audio/)
- Earlier (2023) YouTube figure, reported in Japanese press: pilot creators saw "平均で総再生時間の15%以上が吹替版の言語で再生" (on average over 15% of watch time in dubbed languages) — [Impress Watch](https://www.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1481178.html); the 15% vs 25% discrepancy is noted by [9to5Google (2023)](https://9to5google.com/2023/02/23/youtube-multi-language-audio/) vs the 2025 coverage above.
- Viewer-side mechanics (vendor/press, not official): playback "will automatically default to the viewer's preferred language" and YouTube "may remember" the language preference for future videos; viewers can switch via gear icon > "Audio track" — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-launch-multi-language-audio/); [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-multi-language-audio/)
- YouTube has been testing multi-language thumbnails with select creators since June 2025 (press) — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/10/youtubes-multi-language-audio-feature-for-dubbing-videos-rolls-out-to-all-creators/)
- Official Help: the viewer's **location** setting "impacts the videos that surface for recommendations, trending, and news," whereas language settings change "video metadata, like channel name and video title, when available, and Voice Search language" — [YouTube Help answer/87604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/87604)

### Inferences
- The "all creators" press framing (Sept 2025) and the Help page's "subset with Advanced features / expanding gradually" language coexist as of Oct 2026; the practical rule is: enable Advanced features (phone/ID verification or channel history) and check Studio > Subtitles/Languages for an "Add audio track" option. Treat availability as per-channel.
- The 15–25% non-primary-language watch-time figures are from a hand-picked pilot of mega-channels (MrBeast, Mark Rober, Jamie Oliver); they are an upper bound, not a baseline for a new Japanese channel.
- Since YouTube says recommendations key on location and the dub plays by viewer preference, an English track most plausibly raises retention among English viewers who already got the impression, rather than itself pushing the video into US feeds. No official source confirms either way.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement found on whether a dubbed track changes ranking/recommendation reach (only vendor claims). Could not fetch the full Help page to confirm current eligibility wording or last-updated date.
- No Japan-specific data on multi-language audio uptake.

## KQ2. Auto-dubbing (AI dubbing): languages, Japanese→English, opt in/out, quality

### Takeaway
Japanese→English auto-dubbing is officially supported (Help page table lists English as the only auto-dub target for Japanese-source videos), rollout went to all YPP creators (press: Feb 2026, 27 languages); Japanese does **not** have "expressive speech"; creators can review before publishing or turn it off; YouTube itself warns translations "may not always be accurate."

### Cited Findings
- Official Help "Use automatic dubbing" (answer/15569972): the language table lists "Japanese · English" (Japanese-source videos auto-dub only to English); English-source videos dub to "Arabic, Bengali, Dutch, French*, German*, Hebrew, Hindi*, Indonesian*, Italian*, Japanese, Korean, Malayalam, Polish, Portuguese*, Punjabi, Russian, Spanish*, Tamil, Telugu, Ukrainian" (asterisk = expressive speech; Japanese has no asterisk). Also (excerpt): "As we evaluate and expand support for additional languages, you may see new languages being evaluated marked as 'experimental' in YouTube Studio." — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15569972?hl=en)
- Official YouTube Japan blog (2024-12): auto-dubbing offered to "知識や情報を提供するコンテンツを主に発信する YouTube パートナー プログラムに参加している数十万のチャンネル" with plans to expand; "英語の動画は日本語など8言語に吹き替えでき、日本語を含むこれらの言語の動画は英語に吹き替えできます"; YouTube says "翻訳内容が必ずしも正確ではない場合があります"; dubbed videos carry an "自動吹き替え" label; viewers can switch back to original via track selector — [YouTube Japan Blog](https://blog.youtube/intl/ja-jp/news-and-events/auto-dubbing-on-youtube/); [Impress AV Watch](https://av.watch.impress.co.jp/docs/news/1647038.html)
- Official rollout to all YPP creators (press, 2025): creators can "review all languages" or "experimental languages only" before publishing, or disable auto-dubbing — [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/blog/youtube-auto-dubbing-partner-program/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-expands-auto-dubbing-to-all-creators/811375/)
- Press (Feb 2026): auto-dubbing "now available to all users" with 27 languages; expressive speech list = English, French, German, Hindi, Indonesian, Italian, Portuguese, Spanish (Japanese not included) — [Daily Ittefaq](https://en.ittefaq.com.bd/15221/youtube-rolls-out-auto-dubbing-globally); [Storyboard18](https://www.storyboard18.com/amp/digital/youtube-expands-auto-dubbing-to-27-languages-adds-more-natural-and-expressive-voices-88923.htm)
- Operation (Japanese press, 2024-12): "通常通りに動画をアップロードするだけで特別な操作は不要"; generated dubs appear under Studio > 言語, where they can be unpublished or deleted — [Jetstream blog (JP, secondary)](https://jetstream.blog/2024/12/13/youtube-automatic-multi-language-dubbing-feature/)
- Quality reports: reviewers found some dubs natural and others "ネイティブが話しているようには聞こえない" (not native-sounding) — [Jetstream (JP)](https://jetstream.blog/?p=199617); accuracy affected by accent, background noise, slang, proper names — [Sync.so (vendor)](https://sync.so/blog/youtube-auto-dubbing)
- Lip-sync for dubs is being tested, not shipped (as of mid-2026) — [Uppbeat](https://uppbeat.io/blog/creator-news/youtube-auto-dubbing)
- Creator-side opt-out (third-party 2026 guide): set auto-dubbing to off or to "review before publish" in Studio — [Vozo (vendor)](https://www.vozo.ai/ja/blogs/how-to-turn-off-youtube-auto-dubbing-for-viewers-and-creators)
- Conflicting press: one outlet says creators cannot upload their own dubbed tracks, which contradicts the multi-language-audio Help page above; treat as error — noted in [search results](https://sync.so/blog/youtube-auto-dubbing)

### Inferences
- For a Japanese AI-explainer channel, the realistic auto-dub path is JP→EN only (no JP→Spanish/Hindi etc.), and without expressive speech the English voice will be flat; for a channel whose narration is *already* AI-generated, uploading a separately generated English AI track via multi-language audio (where available) gives more quality control than YouTube's auto-dub.
- "Review before publish" should be on so that mistranslated technical terms (model names, API names) don't ship.

### Gaps
- No creator-reported before/after numbers for Japanese→English auto-dubbing found (JP search returned only feature explainers). Note also that one source says the dub respects the original video's spoken-language setting; check that the video language is set to Japanese or detection may fail.

## KQ3. Subtitles and translated metadata: do they bring overseas views?

### Takeaway
No independent, controlled evidence found; the best data is a 2015 YouTube Creator Blog TED pilot (localized watch time rose from 20% to 35%, up to 60%) and vendor (AIR Media-Tech) case claims of +77% to +195% views after metadata localization. Vendors themselves say speech-heavy content needs subtitles or dubbing, not just titles.

### Cited Findings
- YouTube Creator Blog (2015, TED pilot): translated titles/descriptions paired with translated captions; "localized watchtime increased from 20% to 35% overall, and in several cases up to 60%" — [YouTube Creator Blog UK (Nov 2015)](https://youtubecreator-uk.googleblog.com/2015/11/)
- AIR Media-Tech (localization vendor, self-reported): DIY channel KrasOlka +148% views and +46% revenue after translating into 9 languages; a jazz channel +195% views after 8 languages; a kids channel +77% views over six months after 11 languages, with 70% of new traffic from Home/Suggested; another claim of +131% from 10-language metadata — [AIR: 7 steps to 100k views (2026)](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/7-steps-to-get-100000-views-on-a-translated-youtube-video-in-2026); [AIR best practices](https://air.io/en/best-practices-for-translating-youtube-titles-and-descriptions)
- AIR itself: "metadata translation is not for everyone"; speech-dependent content needs subtitles or dubbing; titles must be adapted, not word-for-word translated — [AIR guide](https://air.io/en/youtube-hacks/how-to-localize-and-translate-youtube-metadata)
- Official Help confirms the viewer language setting swaps in translated title/channel name "when available" — [YouTube Help answer/87604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/87604)
- Automatic captions support Japanese (official list) — [YouTube Help: automatic captioning](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6373554/use-automatic-captioning)
- Listicle evidence that subtitles correlate with overseas reach: Yuka Kinoshita "over 2 billion views – probably helped by the fact that they feature English subtitles"; Kimagure Cook has "a wide international following ... thanks to English-subbed videos" — [Audio Network blog](https://blog.audionetwork.com/the-edit/production/japanese-youtube-stars); [Nihongo Master](https://www.nihongomaster.com/blog/top-10-japanese-youtubers)

### Inferences
- Translated title/description is cheap and is the only thing that changes what an English-locale viewer *sees in the feed*; subtitles alone only help once the click happens. For an explainer (speech-heavy), subs + translated metadata is the minimum; an English audio track is what the vendor and YouTube pilot data actually attach the big numbers to.
- All uplift figures are vendor marketing or decade-old; plan with the assumption that subtitles alone yield a modest, not transformative, overseas share.

### Gaps
- No YouTube statement found quantifying the effect of subtitles alone. No controlled study found.

## KQ5. Policy: Inauthentic content (July 2025) and the July 2026 clarification; what disqualifies AI-narrated explainers

### Takeaway
AI narration is not itself disqualifying. Monetization is lost when a channel's output is "generic, repetitive, or template-based," is "off-putting or distressing," or uses "AI personas" to discuss sensitive topics (finance, legal, health/medical). YouTube's Help text explicitly allows AI as a tool (e.g., "using AI to edit scripts or generate a unique background visual") and bans "AI-generated content built on generic or unoriginal templates that gives an impression of mass production, without the creator's own insights."

### Cited Findings
- Official Help "YouTube channel monetization policies" (answer/1311392): July 2025 rename from "repetitious content" to "inauthentic content"; covers "content that is repetitive or mass-produced"; YouTube says this "content type has always been ineligible"; listed violation (excerpt): "AI-generated content built on generic or unoriginal templates that gives an impression of mass production, without the creator's own insights"; listed acceptable use (excerpt): "Using AI to edit scripts or generate a unique background visual"; "Using AI as a tool is allowed" — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en)
- Reused content is a separate policy (commentary, clips, compilations, reaction) unaffected by the rename — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392?hl=en); [YouTube Community FAQ on reused content](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/271248162/)
- July 2026 clarification (rolled out July 16, 2026, per press; applies to all YPP members). Three categories: (1) "generic, repetitive, or template-based content" — "template-based videos with little original value, including near-identical uploads from one formula"; channels relying heavily on AI, CGI or reusable templates can fall here; "tutorials that closely reproduce widely available material without adding original analysis may also qualify"; (2) "off-putting or distressing content" e.g. staged animal-rescue videos, "regardless of whether AI was used"; (3) "AI personas" discussing "sensitive topics such as finance, legal issues, healthcare, and medical issues" — concern is synthetic representations giving potentially misleading guidance. YouTube's Matt Halprin: "a channel with too much of any of these three types of content will not be able to monetize"; it "is not a wider policy change" — [TechCrunch 2026-07-20](https://techcrunch.com/2026/07/20/youtube-clarifies-policies-around-ai-slop-and-upsetting-videos/); [TechRepublic](https://techrepublic.com/article/news-youtube-ai-video-monetization-rules); [The Desk](https://www.thedesk.net/2026/07/youtube-clamps-down-low-quality-ai-slop/); [Metricool explainer](https://metricool.com/youtube-ai-content-rules-monetization/)
- Enforcement is at channel level (whole-channel review), per secondary reporting — [Flocker](https://flocker.tv/posts/youtube-inauthentic-content-ai-enforcement/)
- Secondary sources list as risky: "AI generated narration paired with stock footage or AI created imagery, published without meaningful human oversight," and the pattern "faceless formats, synthetic voiceovers, templated scripts, and upload schedules built around volume" — [ScaleLab](https://scalelab.com/en/why-youtube-is-cracking-down-on-ai-generated-content-in-2026); [SubSub](https://www.subsub.io/blog/youtube-inauthentic-content-policy-2025)
- YouTube Community FAQ: AI use is permitted if content is "clearly original"; suspended creators can appeal within 21 days (FAQ is ~2.5 years old) — [YouTube Community](https://support.google.com/youtube/community-guide/271248162/)

### Inferences
- Concrete disqualifiers for an AI-narrated AI/tech explainer: formulaic scripts regenerated per topic with no original analysis; "tutorial" videos that reproduce documentation; an AI avatar/persona giving advice on money, law or health (AI investing or "how AI will affect your medical care" scripts with an AI presenter are the highest-risk formats). Keepers of eligibility: original analysis/opinion, hand-built visuals or distinctive animation per video, visible human editorial oversight, varied structure.
- AI topic coverage itself is not a "sensitive topic" under category 3, but AI-finance/AI-health crossovers presented by a synthetic persona are.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch the Help page to quote the full post-July-2026 wording; the three-category framing is confirmed by multiple press outlets but the exact Help Center text is not in hand.

## KQ6. Altered/synthetic content disclosure and 2026 AI-labeling / likeness changes

### Takeaway
Disclosure is required for *realistic* synthetic content (real people saying things they didn't, altered real events/places); non-realistic/animated content and minor edits are exempt. Since May 2026 YouTube auto-applies labels when it detects "significant photorealistic AI use," shows labels below the player (long-form) or as a Shorts overlay, and says a label "alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money." Likeness detection opened to all adult creators (ID + selfie verification).

### Cited Findings
- Official Help "Disclosing use of altered or synthetic content" (answer/14328491): creators set "AI use" under Attributes in Studio; must disclose GenAI content that "makes a real person appear to say or do something they didn't do, and alters footage of a real event or place"; non-realistic content and minor edits (beauty filters, color adjustments) exempt; labels appear "in the video player ... for AI-generated music or photorealistic AI content" and "in the expanded description for non-music AI-generated audio or for non-photorealistic or animated content"; (excerpt) "Creators who consistently choose not to disclose this information may be subject to manual application of a label, or penalties from YouTube, including removal of content or suspension from the YouTube Partner Program." — [YouTube Help answer/14328491](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491?hl=en)
- Official Help "Understanding 'How this content was made' disclosures" (answer/15447836): auto labels based on YouTube's own GenAI tools, C2PA metadata, or internal detection; "You will receive a notification in YouTube Studio if an AI label has been automatically applied"; disclosing AI content alone "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" — [YouTube Help answer/15447836](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en)
- Official Help: separate "Captured with a camera" C2PA label — [YouTube Help answer/15446725](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15446725?hl=en)
- May 2026 update (press): "Beginning in May 2026, YouTube's systems will use new internal signals to identify AI-generated content. If a creator does not specify whether AI was used, but those systems detect significant photorealistic AI use, the platform will automatically apply a label"; long-form label "directly below the video player and above the description," Shorts overlay; "unrealistic, animated, or lightly altered content keeps its disclosure in the expanded description"; Veo/Dream Screen and fully-AI C2PA content labels cannot be removed; others can dispute in Studio; "a disclosure label alone does not change how a video is recommended or whether it's eligible to earn money" — [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/youtube-will-now-automatically-detect-and-label-ai-videos/); [The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/youtube-will-try-to-automatically-flag-ai-videos-starting-this-month/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-introduces-more-prominent-ai-labels/821305/); [TechWyse](https://www.techwyse.com/news/platform-updates/youtube-automatic-ai-labels-disclosure-may-2026)
- Likeness detection expanded to all users 18+ (selfie + ID verification); previously limited to public figures/large creators; still "experimental" and not in every country; YouTube working on an updated privacy process to remove synthetic content simulating an identifiable individual "including their face or voice" — [Exchange4Media](https://www.exchange4media.com/digital-news/youtube-expands-ai-likeness-detection-to-all-adult-users-amid-rising-deepfake-concerns-154745.html); [ID Tech Wire](https://idtechwire.com/youtube-opens-ai-likeness-detection-to-all-creators-aged-18-and-over/)
- Related official page: "Create content for Shorts using AI-generated features" — [YouTube Help answer/15260303](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15260303?hl=en)

### Inferences
- An AI-narrated, AI-assisted *animated/non-photorealistic* explainer generally does not require disclosure under the current rule, and if disclosed the label sits in the expanded description, not on the player. Using photorealistic AI video (e.g., Veo-generated "footage" of real cities/people) triggers the player-level label and, if from YouTube's tools, a permanent one. Cloning a real person's voice without consent exposes the channel to likeness/privacy removals.
- Disclosure is cheap insurance: YouTube says it does not affect reach or monetization, while non-disclosure carries YPP suspension risk.

### Gaps
- No dedicated "AI voice" rule beyond the likeness/privacy process was found; no official page text on whether a synthetic narrator voice (not imitating a real person) must be disclosed — the examples suggest no, but this is inferred.

## KQ7. Monetization thresholds (Oct 2026) and the Feb 2027 change

### Takeaway
Today: 1,000 subs + 4,000 public watch hours (12 months) or 10M Shorts views (90 days) for ads; 500 subs + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views for fan funding/Shopping. From Feb 1, 2027 (confirmed on blog.youtube and Help "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program"): 1,000 subs + 8,000 qualified watch hours (365 days) or 20M qualified Shorts views (90 days) for new applicants; existing members keep status; new activity rule applies.

### Cited Findings
- Official Help "YouTube Partner Program overview & eligibility" (answer/72851): 1,000 subscribers with 4,000 valid public watch hours in the last 12 months or 10M valid public Shorts views in 90 days; Shorts-feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000; fan funding/Shopping tier 500 subs + 3,000 hours or 3M Shorts views — [YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- Official Help "Changes to the YouTube Partner Program" (answer/12843009) and YouTube Blog: starting Feb 1, 2027, ads/Premium entry = 8,000 qualified watch hours in last 365 days or 20M qualified Shorts views in last 90 days, plus 1,000 subscribers; creators already in YPP keep status; "no changes to the eligibility requirements for fan funding, YouTube Creator Partnerships or YouTube Shopping"; from Feb 1, 2027 a channel counts as active with 1,000 qualified watch hours/365 days, or 1M qualified Shorts views/90 days, or uploading 2 long-form videos or 5 Shorts every 90 days; creators must accept updated terms by Jan 31, 2027 or stop earning from affected features on Feb 1 — [YouTube Help answer/12843009](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12843009?hl=en); [YouTube Blog: "New opportunities to earn and changes to the YouTube Partner Program"](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)
- Press corroboration: inactive channels get an extended 90-day window to meet the lower activity threshold; YouTube still reviews channels for policy compliance before approval — [vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/blog/post/youtube-partner-program-changes-2027/); [RouteNote](https://routenote.com/radar/youtube-monetization-requirements-2027-new-ypp-rules-double-watch-hours-and-shorts-views/)

### Inferences
- A channel launching now has roughly three months to reach 1,000 subs + 4,000 hours and be *approved* before Feb 1, 2027; otherwise the bar doubles. Watch hours from an English audience count the same as Japanese ones, so overseas reach directly helps the threshold.
- "Qualified" is new wording; the official pages define it in terms of public, non-Shorts-feed, policy-compliant views — exact definition should be checked on answer/12843009.

### Gaps
- Could not read the full Help page for the definition of "qualified" watch hours.

## KQ8. RPM/CPM for tech/AI education: global vs Japan; does an English audience raise RPM?

### Takeaway
No Japan-specific tech/education RPM data exists in public sources. Country-level estimates (third-party, methodology unclear) put Japan at roughly USD 1.75–3.18 RPM overall vs Tier-1 English markets higher; niche-level vendor estimates put tech CPM at USD 5–30. The claim that English-speaking viewers raise RPM is supported only by the general "US viewers earn 3–5x more per view than India/Brazil" pattern, not by any Japan-vs-US comparison. All figures are estimates.

### Cited Findings
- Dynamoi (third-party aggregator, methodology unclear): Japan AdSense RPM average USD 3.18 per 1,000 views in 2026 (~2.9x its median of USD 1.09); top country Denmark USD 8.38; a separate Dynamoi page gives Japan AdSense RPM USD 1.75 (aggregated/anonymized, through end-2025) and USD 9.83 for music Art Tracks (not relevant to explainers) — [Dynamoi JP data](https://dynamoi.com/data/youtube-adsense-rpm/jp); [Dynamoi RPM Japan](https://dynamoi.com/ko/learn/youtube-music-promotion/youtube-rpm-japan)
- Dynamoi music-channel regional tiers: Tier 1 USD 5.43–9.13 average, with Japan in Tier 1 (music only) — [Dynamoi](https://dynamoi.com/ja/learn/youtube-music-promotion/what-is-a-good-rpm-for-music-channels-on-youtube)

## KQ9. Shorts strategy 2026: how Shorts feed long-form; education data

### Takeaway
Correlational 2026 data (AIR, 18,000 English-language channels, Mar 2025–Mar 2026) puts education among niches where mixing Shorts correlates with more subscribers, recommends ~25–40% Shorts for education and warns above ~55% long-form audiences thin; Metricool (799,718 videos) finds 35+ Shorts/month erases the long-form benefit. Shorts RPM is 3–14% of long-form.

### Cited Findings
- AIR (18,000 English-language channels, March 2025–March 2026): in fitness, food, business and education, mixing Shorts correlates with measurably more subscribers (fitness +41%, business +39%; no separate education figure); recommended education ratio ~25–40% Shorts; stay below ~55% Shorts; findings are correlational — [AIR](https://air.io/en/audience-growth/do-youtube-shorts-help-your-long-form-videos-grow-data-from-18000-channels)
- Metricool (799,718 videos): channels posting 35+ Shorts/month see long-form performance fall back to the level of channels posting none — [Metricool](https://metricool.com/youtube-trends/)
- AIR (274 channels): Shorts RPM 3–14% of long-form RPM in almost every niche — [AIR](https://air.io/en/air-data-findings/youtube-shorts-rpm-vs-long-form-how-much-do-shorts-earn-in-2026)
- Official: Shorts-feed watch hours do not count toward the 4,000-hour threshold; Shorts count via the separate 10M (20M from Feb 2027) views path — [YouTube Help answer/72851](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid)
- Official: May 2026 AI labels appear as an overlay on Shorts — [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-introduces-more-prominent-ai-labels/821305/)
- A ClipSpeed claim of 3–4x subscriber growth from frequent Shorts has no traceable methodology — [ClipSpeed](https://www.clipspeed.ai/blog/how-to-grow-youtube-channel-fast-2026.html)

### Inferences
- For an education channel: Shorts are a discovery/subscriber tool, not revenue; a moderate cadence (roughly 1 Short per 2–3 long-form videos) is what the data supports. Shorts do not help reach the 4,000-hour path.

### Gaps
- No Japan-specific or AI-topic-specific Shorts data; AIR's dataset is English-language channels only.

## KQ10. Does YouTube penalize or favor bilingual (JP audio + EN subs) content? Official statements on language/audience targeting

### Takeaway
No official statement found that YouTube penalizes or boosts bilingual content. YouTube's own Help says location (not language) drives recommendations, and that AI-disclosure labels do not affect recommendation; everything else is inference.

### Cited Findings
- Official Help: location setting "impacts the videos that surface for recommendations, trending, and news"; language setting changes displayed metadata and Voice Search language — [YouTube Help answer/87604](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/87604)
- Official Help: "Change the language of your uploaded video" exists for setting the video's language (affects which captions/dubs are generated) — [YouTube Help answer/6304325](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6304325?hl=en-on)
- Official Help: AI-disclosure label "won't limit a video's audience or impact its eligibility to earn money" — [YouTube Help answer/15447836](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15447836?hl=en)
- Third-party claims that YouTube pushes videos in the viewer's preferred language exist but are not backed by any Google statement — [search results summary](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/87604)
- Vendor (ShortsFast) discussion of whether English and Spanish need separate channels — relevant framing but not official — [ShortsFast](https://shortsfast.com/blog/decide-whether-english-and-spanish-need-separate-channels/)

### Inferences
- Nothing indicates a penalty for bilingual content. The practical risk is audience-signal dilution: early viewer retention from one locale shapes who the video is tested on; a JP-audio video with EN subs will initially be tested on Japanese viewers. Multi-language audio plus translated metadata is YouTube's own mechanism for one video serving both locales; separate English-first uploads or a second channel is the alternative if retention among English viewers lags.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube statement on language-based recommendation targeting beyond location was found; the "one channel vs two channels" question has no official guidance.
