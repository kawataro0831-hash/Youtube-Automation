## Key question 1 — Channel-by-channel reference list (numbers, cadence, style, verdict)

### Takeaway
Of the 24 channels below, the three cleanest models for a calm, learning-oriented Japanese animated channel are **3Blue1Brown** (8.4–8.5M subs, programmatic animation, ~241 videos in 11 years), **Branch Education** (2.75M subs, 3D models that take months per video) and **Welch Labs** (~0.84–0.89M subs, ~415K avg views per video, i.e. the highest views-per-subscriber ratio in the set). The news-reaction lane (Matt Wolfe ~1.0M, AI Daily Brief ~583K with 1,400+ videos, Matthew Berman ~0.53–0.63M, Wes Roth ~0.32M) grows on volume and speed, not per-video depth, and is structurally incompatible with a low-cadence animated channel.

### Inferences (format characterizations are UNSOURCED — from prior familiarity; verify by watching)
- **Fireship**: 100-second explainers plus "The Code Report" news; rapid cuts, meme stills, dry deadpan narration, code on screen; titles are short noun phrases ("X in 100 Seconds", "X is a mistake"). *Borrow*: the discipline of a fixed-length format and a recognizable title template. *Don't borrow*: the joke density and irony — it reads as flippant in Japanese and is tied to one author's voice.
- **Two Minute Papers**: paper-demo footage + enthusiastic voiceover, catchphrases ("What a time to be alive!", "Hold on to your papers"), 5–10 min, ~2/week. *Borrow*: ritual phrases that make the channel feel like a club (cheap, effective). *Don't borrow*: the relentless excitement; the cited criticism (abstract-paraphrasing, no mechanism) is exactly what a learning channel should avoid.
- **AI Explained**: slides/screenshots, measured British narration, reads the actual papers/system cards, 15–30 min, ~weekly. *Borrow*: "I read it so you don't have to, and here is what is actually new" framing; calm skepticism; building your own small benchmark/test as a recurring segment. *Don't borrow*: the dense wall-of-text screenshots — they do not translate well to animation.
- **Matt Wolfe / Berman / Wes Roth / AI Daily Brief**: talking head over browser tabs, 2–7 uploads/week, "This changes everything"-style titles, red arrows and shocked-face thumbnails. *Don't borrow* anything structural: their growth comes from upload volume and recency, which an animated channel cannot match, and the hype register conflicts with a calm brand. *Borrow* only: their habit of a clear "what happened → why it matters → what to do" three-beat ordering.
- **bycloud**: edited talking-head + paper figures + memes, ~10–15 min. Plateaued growth suggests the "fun paper explainer" niche is saturated in English.
- **3Blue1Brown**: opens with a concrete puzzle or a wrong intuition, builds the mechanism visually step by step, pauses to invite the viewer to predict, closes by connecting to a larger idea; no face, minimal music, 15–30 min; title pattern "But what is X?" / "Why do X?". *Borrow*: the opening-with-a-question, the "predict before I show you" beat, the no-face format, chapter-series structure. *Don't borrow* without resources: the full mathematical rigor per video (weeks of animation per video).
- **Welch Labs**: hand-drawn/Manim visuals plus physical props, slow narration, a historical "how we got here" spine, 15–25 min, roughly 2–3/month. *Borrow*: the history-of-an-idea spine, which is exactly the structure Japanese 教養 audiences already like; the narrow catalog with very high per-video views proves depth beats volume.
- **Kurzgesagt**: flat-vector bird mascots, warm narrator, "Imagine…" openings, 8–15 min, consistent end-card with sources link, sponsor read mid/late. *Borrow*: source-list transparency, mascot continuity, bright-but-calm palette, "we are many videos in parallel" pipeline. *Don't borrow*: 1,200 hours/video — unattainable for a small team; also their topic breadth (existential risk, space) rather than mechanism.
- **ColdFusion**: slow, cinematic, archival footage, ambient music, narrator-led tech/business history, 15–25 min. *Borrow*: the calm pacing and cinematic restraint — the closest "tone match" to a calm Japanese channel in this list.
- **Wendover / PolyMatter**: motion-graphics maps and diagrams, numbers-driven arguments, 15–25 min. *Borrow*: diagram-first visual grammar and economic framing. PolyMatter's upload gaps show the cost of this style for a solo producer.
- **Johnny Harris**: on-camera host, maps, documentary b-roll, strong emotional hooks. *Don't borrow*: host-centric persona; it conflicts with a no-face animated format.

## Key question 3 — Structure and production of top mechanism explainers (3Blue1Brown, Welch Labs, Branch Education) and disclosed workflows

### Takeaway
Only 3Blue1Brown and Kurzgesagt disclose quantified workflows: Sanderson budgets under a week of animation per ~10 minutes using his own programmatic library Manim (open-sourced, Manim Community fork for beginners), and Kurzgesagt reports ≥1,200 hours per video in After Effects with 2–3 animators over 8–10 weeks. ### Cited Findings

- 3Blue1Brown FAQ page exists at [3blue1brown.com/faq](https://www.3blue1brown.com/faq/) (could not be opened this session)
- Welch Labs started using Manim with a recorded 1:1 session from Sanderson, 26 Nov 2024, for the Schrödinger-equation video — [3Blue1Brown members post](https://members.3blue1brown.com/posts/manim-session-116778481?l=en-GB)

### Inferences
- A realistic solo/small-team budget, scaling from Sanderson's figure: ~1 week of animation per 10 minutes → a 15-minute mechanism video ≈ 1.5 weeks animation + script/research, i.e. 2–3 uploads/month is the ceiling for a Manim-style channel; Kurzgesagt-level polish (1,200 h) is 5–10× that and not a model for a start-up channel.
- Manim (Python) is the most "borrowable" toolchain: open source, has a Japanese-speaking community, and its output style is already associated with trustworthy math/AI explanation. Branch Education-style 3D is a far larger per-video investment (months).
- Structural pattern shared by 3B1B and Welch Labs (unsourced characterization, verify): (1) open with a concrete question or misconception, (2) build the mechanism one visual primitive at a time, (3) explicit "pause and predict" moments, (4) close by zooming out to why this matters / what comes next in the series. Series numbering ("chapter 5") improves binge-watching and should be copied.

## Key question 4 — AI voices / AI visuals and audience reaction

### Takeaway
None of the 24 channels above is documented in this session as using an AI narrator; the strongest signal is the opposite — Kurzgesagt built a 12-minute video around "AI Slop Is Killing Our Channel" (Oct 2025). Survey and policy evidence says audiences penalize generic/undisclosed synthetic narration and mass-produced content (Animoto 2025: 78% trust real-person videos more; YouTube removed 16 channels/35M subs in Jan 2026), while tolerating AI voices on original informational content.

### Cited Findings
- Kurzgesagt "AI Slop Is Killing Our Channel", 7 Oct 2025, 12 min, on AI-generated content flooding the internet — [TheTVDB](https://thetvdb.com/series/kurzgesagt-in-a-nutshell/episodes/11383775)
- Animoto survey (Sept 2025, via StudyFinds): 78% of US consumers trust videos with real people more than AI-generated content; 36% of those who watched AI-generated video said it lowered their trust in the brand — [Craftomorrow summary](https://craftomorrow.com/ai-voice-vs-your-own-voice/)
- YouTube terminated 16 mass-produced channels (35M combined subs) in January 2026 under the inauthentic-content policy — [InVideo blog citing The Next Web](https://invideo.io/blog/youtube-kills-ai-faceless-channels/)
- Comment backlash is driven by "the same voice as every other channel" (recognition fatigue) more than by audio quality — [SpeakSay blog, 2026](https://www.speaksay.com/blog/ai-voice-fatigue-comment-backlash-unique-voice-2026)
- Vendor guides claim viewers "don't mind AI narration for informational content" / are "unlikely to notice" on tutorials and explainers — [NarrationBox](https://narrationbox.com/blog/3-mistakes-to-avoid-while-using-ai-voice-for-youtube-2026); [Rekam](https://www.rekam.ai/blog/best-ai-voices-for-youtube-videos) (vendor marketing, low reliability)
- Disclosure debate: arguments for disclosing AI narration centre on trust and avoiding backlash on discovery — [Craftomorrow](https://craftomorrow.com/ai-voice-vs-your-own-voice/)

### Inferences
- For a Japanese channel that will likely use TTS (the repo's own pipeline uses Gemini TTS), the risk is not the voice per se but (a) sounding like every other 「ゆっくり/VOICEVOX」 channel and (b) undisclosed use. A distinctive, consistently-used voice plus a one-line disclosure in the description is the defensible position; Kurzgesagt's public anti-slop stance shows top animated channels are positioning *against* generic AI output, so visuals should be clearly authored (Manim/vector), not image-model stills.

### Gaps
